Keeper's handbook

How to play

Your job

Ships are making for the harbour at the foot of your lighthouse. Each night a set number are due in. Bring them all in and you see the dawn, then the next night is busier. Three wrecks ends your watch.

The beam

In the fog, a ship can only see the rocks while your beam is on it, and for a second or so after. Out of the light it drifts off course and sails blind. You can always see ships' lanterns through the fog, so you know where to point.

The wide beam lights a broad area close to the island. The focused beam is narrow but reaches the edge of the sea.

Wrecks and second chances

You can wreck three ships. The third ends your watch and you start again from night 1. The wreck markers at the top show how many you have left.

The harbour board can clear wrecks from your record at dawn:

  • Every third night (nights 3, 6, 9 and on) clears one wreck.
  • A night with no wrecks, from night 4 on, clears one wreck too.

You never have more than three chances. If your record is already clean, you get a commendation instead, shown when your watch ends.

Upgrades

At each dawn the supply boat brings three upgrades and you choose one. It stays for the rest of your watch, and the game waits while you choose (press 1, 2 or 3 on a keyboard).

Wider lensThe wide beam covers 10% more sea. Up to three times.
Brighter lampThe wide beam reaches 8% farther. Up to three times.
Better chartsShips remember the rocks 0.3 seconds longer. Up to three times.
FoghornOnce a night (twice at level II), press H or tap Foghorn and every ship can see the rocks for 3 seconds.
Lifeboat crewYour next wreck is rescued and does not count. You hold one at a time, and the boat brings at most three a watch.
Sweeping lampA second, dimmer lamp turns on its own and lights ships as it passes.

Daily voyage

Each day brings one voyage that everyone sails: the same rocks, the same ships in the same order, and the same weather. The supply boat offers everyone the same upgrades too. Play it as often as you like; your best run of the day counts. A new voyage starts at midnight UTC. Rocks are fitted to your screen, so the layout only matches exactly on screens of the same shape.

Harbour board

The Today tab ranks the best runs on today's voyage. The harbour board on the title and game-over screens lists the ten best watches from everyone who plays this lighthouse. Your entry updates whenever you beat your own best. Adding a score needs you to be signed in to Claude with an account that can change this game's shared data; anyone else can still play, and their best is kept on their own device.

The tide

The tide gauge at the top rises and falls over about 45 seconds. When it falls, rocks break the surface and get bigger. Faint ripples in your beam mark rocks just under the water. They are safe for now but will surface as the tide drops. Most wrecks happen at low water.

Reading the dark

  • A steady amber lantern is a ship sailing normally.
  • A flickering red lantern means a ship in the dark is about to hit a rock. Get the beam on it fast.
  • A lantern that only blinks now and then is a smuggler.

Weather

Night 1 is always calm. From night 2, weather rolls in every minute or so and lasts about 20 seconds. A message at the top says what is coming.

SquallStrong wind pushes ships off course. Ships in your beam fight it better.
Fog bankYour beam does not reach as far.
ThunderstormWind and rain. Lightning lights the whole sea for a moment, so use it to spot rocks.
MoonlightA break. The fog thins and ships hold their course better.

Distress flares

From night 2, a crew in trouble sometimes sends up a red flare out at sea, and a message says which way to look. Hold your beam on it for 2.5 seconds before it burns out, about 12 seconds later. A green ring fills while your beam is on it, and a faint ring shows how long the flare has left.

A rescued crew counts as one ship brought in, for the night and for your score. Missing a flare costs nothing, but it takes your beam away from the ships, so choose your moment. Far-off flares need the focused beam.

Tips

  • Listen to the music. It grows tenser when the sea gets dangerous, and a drum joins in when things are getting bad.
  • Sweep instead of staring. A quick pass over a ship is enough to show it the rocks ahead.
  • Deal with red lanterns first. Everything else can wait a second.
  • Ships near the island are safe. Spend your light on the ones far out.
  • Keep an eye on the tide gauge. When it starts falling, the sea gets crowded with rocks.
